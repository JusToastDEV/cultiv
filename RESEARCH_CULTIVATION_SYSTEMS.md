# Cultivation Systems Research Notes

## Scope
This document summarizes high-level system patterns from popular cultivation fiction and related genre references.
It is intended for gameplay design adaptation, not direct content copying.

## Reliable System Patterns Across Popular Works
1. Hierarchical progression ladders
- Distinct major realms with multiple internal levels are nearly universal.
- Each breakthrough gates new abilities and social status.
- Many systems attach lifespan growth to higher realms.

2. Scarcity and competition loops
- Rare resources (flames, herbs, relics, manuals, inheritances) drive conflict.
- Sects, clans, and alliances compete to monopolize breakthrough resources.
- Auctions and black markets are major progression accelerators.

3. Technique-based identity
- Characters are often defined by a signature path (alchemy, sword, body, spirit beasts, etc.).
- Top systems avoid pure stat inflation and instead unlock new interactions.

4. Breakthrough risk and backlash
- Failed advancement frequently causes injury, regression, qi deviation, or lifespan cost.
- High-power methods usually carry debt (karma, instability, social threat).

5. Faction politics and geography
- Political structures matter: sect courts, clan hierarchies, imperial powers, hidden domains.
- Progression is tied to moving through stronger regions and institutions.

6. Companion systems and bound entities
- Several popular works include companion entities, spirits, or summons with growth trees.
- Companion progression often parallels the main character progression.

7. Alchemy/crafting as strategic progression
- Alchemy and forging are not side jobs; they are central to economy and power.
- Craft paths produce utility spikes: pills, detox, breakthrough support, artifact refinement.

## Pain Points Seen in Reader Feedback
Patterns in public reader reactions suggest key pitfalls:
- Arc repetition where each zone repeats the same humiliation-revenge ladder.
- Overly linear power creep with no meaningful build tradeoffs.
- Endless chapter padding without systemic novelty.

Design response for this project:
- Force event consequences and delayed outcomes.
- Make each region/capital mechanically unique.
- Add cross-path synergies and conflicts to prevent one solved meta.

## Adaptation Guidance for This Web Game
1. Keep realm ladders clear and finite in each release.
2. Tie lifespan to quality of growth, not just raw level.
3. Make factions and city laws alter what is legal, profitable, and safe.
4. Ensure inventory/storage logistics influence exploration choices.
5. Treat alchemy/craft/spirit systems as equal progression paths, not optional flavor.
6. Use inheritance events as major branch points with permanent consequences.

## Copyright and Originality Guardrails
- Do not reuse protected character names, sect names, or unique title-specific artifacts.
- Do not recreate one-to-one plot arcs from any single series.
- Use references as mechanical inspiration only.

## Immediate Design Additions Suggested
- Build an event tagging matrix for region x faction x cultivation path.
- Add companion spirit contracts with risk of betrayal/corruption.
- Add city permit systems for pill shops, forging halls, and black market access.
- Add breakthrough preparation phase with optional safeguards and costs.

## Currency Patterns To Adapt
Observed cross-series and glossary patterns support multi-currency economies instead of one coin:

1. Mortal currency
- Copper, silver, and gold used by common markets and low-tier cities.
- Works for housing, food, transport, and legal fees.

2. Cultivator currency
- Spirit stones are repeatedly used as high-tier trade currency among cultivators.
- Practical uses: market bidding, array fueling, direct cultivation refill, and artifact charging.

3. Faction currency
- Contribution points, merit tokens, or sect credits are usually non-transferable.
- Typical sinks: manuals, internal treasury access, elder favors, mission rewards.

4. Rare barter currencies
- Beast cores, blood essences, and relic shards often behave like commodity money.
- Better for black market and emergency trading than fixed coin values.

5. Design recommendation for this project
- Keep silver as baseline city currency.
- Introduce spirit stones for realm-tier progression and advanced purchases.
- Add guild contribution points for regulated rewards that cannot be bought directly.
